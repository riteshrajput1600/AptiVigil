# AptiVigil website and competitor notes

Research checked on 18 October 2026. This is a product-positioning reference, not a claim that AptiVigil already provides these competitor capabilities.

## Public competitor patterns

| Provider | Public product/pricing pattern observed | What AptiVigil can learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Honorlock](https://honorlock.com/exclusive-proctoring-features/) | Promotes flat-rate pricing per exam or per student, but does not show a public numeric price on the reviewed page. Its public pages emphasize a demo and institution-specific discussion. | Explain what affects a quote and avoid time-based cost surprises if the eventual service model can support predictable pricing. |
| [Meazure Learning / ProctorU](https://www.meazurelearning.com/exam-technology/proctoru-online-proctoring) | Presents service lines for different exam stakes and combines software with professional review/support. The page asks institutions to request a demo rather than publishing list prices. | Separate lower-stakes and higher-stakes workflows by service scope, and describe what human review/support includes. |
| [Mercer | Mettl](https://mettl.com/remote-exam-monitoring-and-invigilation/) | Positions online exams, platform-based AI/human proctoring and proctoring-as-a-service for institutional use cases; promotes a demo. The reviewed page does not publish list pricing. | Show complete exam use cases and integration options, while keeping custom implementation and availability clear. |
| [Eklavvya](https://www.eklavvya.com/) | Uses broad navigation grouped by assessment features, education/corporate audiences, resources and help; separates product capabilities and use cases. Its pricing page presents Free/Professional/Enterprise tiers and a feature matrix, with annual exam credits (one candidate sitting one exam uses one credit). The Free tier is listed with a limited credit allowance; paid scope depends on volume/modules and is quote-led. | Organize discovery around audience and capability, compare packages clearly, and explain any usage unit. AptiVigil should not copy Eklavvya's free tier because the agreed sales model has no public self-signup. |

The reviewed pages do not provide enough paid price data to establish an apples-to-apples price benchmark. Eklavvya lists a limited free allowance, but its paid plans are scoped around volume and enabled modules; Honorlock describes per-exam/per-student flat-rate options; Meazure uses service lines and a demo-led sales path. Pricing can depend on volume, exam duration, service level, human review, implementation, support and integration scope. AptiVigil's public pricing page therefore shows conceptual packages without numeric rates and sends institutions to a quote discussion. Do not publish competitor prices or invent AptiVigil rates.

## Proctoring page messaging reviewed

- [Eklavvya remote proctoring](https://www.eklavvya.com/remote-proctoring/) organizes its page around identity verification, continuous monitoring, exam access, AI analysis, live proctor support and reports. It advertises image, video and automated proctoring, plus education and corporate use cases.
- [Mercer | Mettl online remote proctoring](https://mettl.com/en/online-remote-proctoring/) describes pre-exam identity checks and during-exam AI flags, human proctoring and secure-browser controls. Its feature sections include dual-camera, audio and record-and-review options.
- [Meazure Learning / ProctorU](https://www.meazurelearning.com/exam-technology/proctoru-online-proctoring) separates service levels and explains human support and professional review. Its [Record+ page](https://www.meazurelearning.com/exam-technology/proctoru-online-proctoring/record-plus) says a flag is not an incident report until a professional proctor reviews and confirms it against exam rules.
- Competitor feature copy often claims live, accurate, certified or production-ready performance. AptiVigil has no implementation or validation evidence for those claims yet. Its site now describes identity-photo comparison, multiple-face/face-absence, attention, voice-activity, phone/object and browser signals as planned capabilities, with a human-review step and explicit browser limitations.

## Website pages added

- **Solutions**: school groups, higher education, certification/training and assessment providers.
- **Services**: assessment delivery, structured/live/coding interviews, monitoring and human review.
- **Packages**: capability groups without fabricated price claims.
- **Pricing**: quote factors and a demo/quote CTA; no unvalidated rates.
- **Security & privacy**: privacy principles, browser limitations and a prominent statement that the current scaffold is not production-ready or certified.
- **About**: product intent without invented company history or customer claims.
- **FAQ**: account provisioning, login readiness, pricing, exam formats and monitoring limits.
- **Contact/demo** and **Log in**: sales-led prospect intake and the common customer entry point.

Eklavvya's homepage also uses customer logos, testimonials, usage totals, security certifications, performance outcomes and case studies. Those are effective proof sections only when supported by real customer permission and independently verified evidence. AptiVigil currently has no such proof and should not use dummy numbers, copied testimonials, competitor logos or unearned certification badges.

## Claims to keep out until verified

- Customer counts, uptime, detection accuracy, certifications, compliance badges or integration logos.
- Numeric per-seat/per-exam rates before infrastructure, human-review and support costs are known.
- Statements that AI determines cheating or that a normal browser can identify other apps/processes.
- Claims that current authentication, proctoring, coding execution or production security are complete.
